**Title: Our $500 Robot Had ZERO Brain Cells 💀**

Our robotics team spent an entire afternoon trying to teach a $500 robot how to do the one thing it was built for.

Drive in a straight line.

And somehow, it was losing.

We programmed it to drive forward six feet. Easy, right?

Wrong.

The robot would drive forward, overshoot the target, reverse, overshoot again, and start shaking like it was having an actual mental breakdown.

Our builder blamed the wheels.

Our programmer blamed the motors.

And one guy genuinely suggested that the floor was uneven.

So, naturally, we spent another hour changing absolutely everything except the actual problem.

Then our coach walked over, watched the robot zigzag across the room, and asked one question.

"Have you guys tuned your PID?"

Silence.

Turns out, our robot was correcting itself WAY too aggressively.

The proportional gain was so high that every tiny error made it overreact. It would turn too far, realize it messed up, and immediately overcorrect in the opposite direction.

Basically, our robot had the driving skills of a sleep-deprived teenager.

So we started tuning the PID values.

Lowered the P gain. Tested it.

Still overshooting.

Adjusted the D gain. Tested it again.

And suddenly, the robot drove almost perfectly straight.

WE ACTUALLY DID IT.

The entire team started celebrating like we'd just won the world championship.

Then we ran our autonomous routine.

The robot drove perfectly to its target, turned exactly ninety degrees, and lined itself up beautifully.

And then it launched itself directly into a wall.

Because we forgot to reverse one motor's direction.

All that PID tuning...

Just for our robot to discover a brand-new way to embarrass us.
