Our intern fixed a six-month bug in one afternoon
I was working at a company that built scheduling software. Every few days, certain appointments would randomly appear an hour earlier than they should.
We tried everything. From rewriting parts of the scheduling system, to adding breakpoints, everything. Nothing was working consistently.
Then a new intern joined our team.
During his first week, he asked why we stored appointment times without their time zones.
One of the senior developers laughed and said the system had worked that way for years.
The intern spent an afternoon looking through the code and found the problem.
We were saving times in one format, then interpreting them in another whenever daylight saving time changed.
Since the bug was only for some customers, it was very hard to find and reproduce the issue.
He wrote a small fix, added a few tests, and submitted a pull request soon after.
It passed the review the next morning.
The bug was gone.
He finished his internship three months later.
We offered him a full-time job before he graduated.
