Our new junior IT technician solved a six-month server outage mystery during her first coffee break.

I was working at a regional healthcare network that managed patient portals across three states. Every single Tuesday at precisely two in the afternoon, our main authentication server would crash for twenty minutes, locking thousands of doctors and nurses out of patient records.

We tried everything. We upgraded our firewall, replaced the server hardware, and even brought in cybersecurity specialists to check for DDoS attacks. Nothing explained the weekly crashes.

Then a new junior technician joined our infrastructure team.

On her second day, she asked why the server room's automated climate control was synced to the building's main breakroom circuit.

One of the senior engineers laughed and told her that was just a wiring quirk from when the facility was renovated five years ago.

The technician spent her coffee break checking the building's power logs against our server crash timestamps and spotted the issue immediately.

Every Tuesday afternoon, the cafeteria staff ran an industrial commercial microwave to prep meals for the evening shift. Because the microwave and the server room's backup cooling unit shared an outdated breaker, the power surge briefly tripped the cooling unit's voltage regulator.

The server rack's thermal safety switch detected the two-second drop in airflow and automatically shut down the system to prevent overheating.

Because the cooling unit immediately restarted on its own, no alarms ever went off by the time engineers walked down to check the room.

She rerouted the cooling unit to a dedicated backup generator circuit, updated the power monitoring alerts, and handed over a diagram of the fix before lunch.

We tested the breaker load that afternoon.

The Tuesday crashes stopped completely.

She finished her training period a month early, and we promoted her to lead systems architect before the end of the year.
